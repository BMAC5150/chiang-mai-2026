Red Flags

Avoid any venue offering
Non-negotiable

Walk away if you see

  • ×Elephant riding — any form, including bareback.
  • ×Shows and tricks — painting, dancing, or circus-style performances.
  • ×Visible chains or bullhooks (except in absolute emergency control).
  • ×Large group bathing — often masks punishment-based training to keep animals compliant around crowds.

Practical Planning

Booking & what to wear
Small logistics

Before you go

  • Book early. The popular ethical sanctuaries fill up fast — book at least several weeks in advance.
  • Clothing. Wear comfortable, thick cotton trousers that cover your knees — this avoids "elephant rash" from their spiky hair. Bring a change of clothes and a towel if participating in water activities.
  • Hygiene. You'll be required to wash off all sunscreen and bug repellent before interacting with the animals — this protects their skin and the ecosystem.
The reasoned pick

For a first Chiang Mai visit, the top-tier observation-only options (ChangChill, BEES) offer the clearest ethical footing. If you'd like more contact, Kanta keeps groups small and stress low.
