Wat Sri Suphan · The Silver Temple
Walkable
Wua Lai Road ~2.2 km · 5-min Grab

An extraordinary temple completely embellished in hand-tooled silver and aluminum alloy, showcasing the neighborhood's deep metalworking heritage.

The grounds run alongside the Saturday Wua Lai Walking Street. The interior of the main silver hall is traditionally restricted to men — the spectacular exterior can be admired by all.
Wat Chedi Luang
Walkable
Phra Pok Klao Rd · central Old City ~2.0 km · 7-min Grab

Built in the 14th–15th centuries, this temple features a colossal brick chedi partially ruined by an earthquake. It once housed Thailand's most sacred image, the Emerald Buddha.

Wat Phan Tao · Teak Hall
Walkable
Adjacent to Wat Chedi Luang ~2.0 km · 7-min Grab

A beautiful, quiet contrast to its massive neighbor. The main prayer hall is constructed entirely of dark, polished teak wood panels, with an elegant golden Lanna peacock carved above the entrance.

Wat Inthakhin · City Pillar
Short ride
Near Three Kings Monument ~2.2 km · 8-min Grab

A small, intimate temple that houses the sacred Inthakhin (city pillar) — believed by locals to protect Chiang Mai and bring stability.

Wat Phra Singh
Short ride
Western end of Ratchadamnoen Rd ~2.5 km · 9-min Grab

One of Chiang Mai's grandest and most respected temples. Classic layered Lanna roofs, magnificent golden details, and home to the revered Phra Singh Buddha image.

Wat Chiang Man · The Oldest
Short ride
Ratchaphakhinai Rd · NE Old City ~2.5 km · 9-min Grab

Chiang Mai's oldest temple, founded in the late 13th century by King Mengrai during the city's construction. Famous for a distinctive chedi supported by rows of carved stone elephants.

Wat Pha Lat · Hidden Forest
Day trip
Shaded slopes of Doi Suthep ~8 km · 15–20 min drive

A peaceful sanctuary that blends seamlessly into the jungle. Reached on foot via the historic Monk's Trail or by car — moss-covered stone structures, hidden shrines, and a running stream.

Also covered on the Hikes page as the trail-end reward.
Wat Phra That Doi Suthep
Day trip
Doi Suthep summit ~15 km · 35–40 min drive

Chiang Mai's most famous and sacred mountaintop temple. Climb the 306-step Naga serpent staircase (or take the cable car) to a gleaming golden chedi, soft prayer bells, and a panoramic view of the valley below.

Go early — before 9 AM — for cool mountain mist and to beat the crowds.
Wat Phra That Doi Saket
Day trip
Doi Saket District · NE on Hwy 118 ~18 km · 25–30 min drive

A majestic hill temple that rivals the city's famous spots in scale and intricate murals, but remains remarkably quiet and free from tourist buses.

Temple Etiquette

Small courtesies that matter
Respect the room

Five golden rules

  • Dress modestly. Shoulders and knees covered — men and women. Skip sleeveless tops, tank tops, and short shorts. Lightweight cotton or linen is coolest in the humidity.
  • Remove your shoes. Always slip them off before stepping inside indoor prayer halls (viharns) — a sign of physical and spiritual cleanliness.
  • Step over, not on. When entering doorways, step over the raised wooden or stone threshold — local spirits are traditionally believed to reside there.
  • Watch your feet. When sitting inside, never point the soles of your feet toward a Buddha image, a monk, or another worshipper. Sit cross-legged or kneel with toes back.
  • Body language. Try not to turn your back to a Buddha image when leaving or posing for photos. Public displays of affection are strictly out of place here.
A quiet-hours nod. Most temples welcome visitors from roughly 6 AM to 6 PM. Early mornings are cooler, softer, and largely to yourself — chants often still drifting from the halls.
