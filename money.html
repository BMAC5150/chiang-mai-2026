The Perfect-Bill Rule
  • Pristine. Crisp, new, and untorn. Even a microscopic tear = immediate rejection.
  • Unmarked. No ink, no stamps, no teller notations of any kind.
  • Denominations. $50 and $100 bills only — smaller notes attract the worst rates.
  • Exchange in the city, not the airport. Boutiques like SuperRich beat kiosk rates dramatically.

The DCC Trap

Critical alert
Dynamic Currency Conversion
Critical · Mandatory

Never let the ATM convert for you.

The machine will offer to “lock in” an exchange rate by charging your account in USD. Refuse it. This is Dynamic Currency Conversion — a high-markup trap the local bank uses to bypass your Chase benefits.

Withdraw without conversion
Debit in THB
The bottom line. Select THB and Chase handles the conversion at the mid-market rate. Select USD and the local bank slaps on a 5–10% markup that Chase cannot reimburse.

Daily Habits

Small moves, big compounding
Muscle memory for the trip

Three habits, every day

  1. The 7-Eleven Maneuver ATMs dispense ฿1,000 notes — vendors often can't break them. Right after a withdrawal, buy a small item at 7-Eleven to break the bills into 20s, 50s, and 100s — the “small-ball” currency the city runs on.
  2. Strategic withdrawal amounts Stick to ฿5,000–฿10,000 per pull. Enough to minimize ATM visits, small enough to keep your on-person cash manageable.
  3. The Locked Reserve The $200–$300 USD stays in the hotel safe. Only bring it out if the electronic system fails and you're heading to a city exchange booth.
