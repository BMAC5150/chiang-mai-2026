Health & Vaccinations

Chiang Mai · September
Before you go
Nothing required to enter

No mandatory vaccines for arrivals from the US. Yellow fever proof is only needed if arriving from a risk country in Africa or South America — your route doesn't apply.

Recommended
Routine, up to date— MMR / measles, Tdap, polio
Hepatitis A— advised for most travelers
Typhoid— lots of street food / longer stay
Hep B · Rabies · JE— activity-dependent
Worth confirmingSituational
Rainy season → mosquitoes

September is green season. Dengue is the main concern and has no traveler's prophylaxis, so bite prevention is the defense — DEET or picaridin repellent, day and evening, since the mosquito bites in daylight too.

Malaria — not a city concern

Risk sits in remote border provinces, not Chiang Mai city or the usual sights. Antimalarials generally aren't advised unless you add rural border trekking.

One travel-clinic visit

6–8 weeks before you fly lets multi-dose vaccines take full effect. Departing early September from a June start, you have comfortable lead time.

General guidance, not personal medical advice. A travel-medicine clinic can tailor this to each traveler's history and plans. Sources: CDC & WHO travelers' health.
